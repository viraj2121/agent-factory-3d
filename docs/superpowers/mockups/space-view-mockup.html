What holds the sky view back today

The sky is drawn by code

Noise and dots never read as real space. It ends up a flat dark blue with snow on it.

Flat light

Every island is lit evenly from all sides: no bright side, no night side, no shape.

Blurry brown disks

The "rocks" are flat pictures facing the camera, so they look like smudges, not stone.

Thick lines with white dots

The links look like a chart drawn over the picture, not something that exists in space.

Six upgrades

Stage A

1A real sky photo

  • A real high-resolution Milky Way panorama wrapped around the scene: true star colours, dust lanes, depth.
  • A nearby star as the sun, a ringed gas giant as the big anchor, one or two moons.
  • The three themes stay as three sky photos plus their own planet: Deep space, Nebula, Aurora planet.
Speed: about the same or faster. One image on one sphere, in place of today's star shader. Size: one 4K image, compressed for the graphics card.
Stage A

2Film lighting

  • One hard sunlight: every island gets a lit side, a night side and a rim of light from the planet.
  • Film-like colour, a lens flare from the sun, fine grain, darker corners.
  • Far islands fade a little into the dark, so near and far read at a glance.
Speed: small. One extra screen pass; shadows already exist. Lite keeps the light but drops flare, grain and fade.
Stage B

3Islands become stations

  • New bases built in Blender: cracked rock chunks with strata and a metal docking ring, not round blobs.
  • Real debris: small rocks orbiting each island, real shapes lit by the sun.
  • Status as behaviour: thrusters and blinking docking lights while agents work, a beacon beam when it needs you, dim and drifting when idle.
Speed: neutral. One merged model per island, and all debris drawn in one call (instancing).
Stage B

5Orbit layout with depth

  • The current project is the central station. Others ride tilted orbits around it at different distances and heights.
  • Busier projects sit on the inner orbit, closer and bigger; resting ones further out.
  • Clean labels: thin hologram names for the current project, anything that needs you, and whatever you hover. No boxes.
Speed: none. Only positions change. Also removes the 8-island cap: far orbits can hold more.
Stage C

4Space traffic

  • Thin glowing energy filaments that branch like a tree from the current station, replacing thick lines.
  • Small shuttles fly along the filaments, more when that project's agents are busy, none when it rests.
  • A filament to a project that needs you pulses amber toward you.
Speed: small. All shuttles drawn in one call. Decoration only: a shuttle carries no real task.
Stage C

6Warp jump and camera

  • Picking a project: stars stretch into streaks, a flash, and you arrive at the other station.
  • The sky view drifts slowly like a film camera; the mouse adds a little parallax.
  • Picture mode becomes a proper wallpaper shot of this view.
Speed: none at rest. The streak effect runs only during the 1.5 s jump. Reduced motion skips it for a plain fade.

How we would build it

Stage A · sky and light

Biggest jump, no Blender

Sky photo per theme, sun, gas giant with rings, film lighting, flare, grain, distance fade. You check it in the browser before Stage B.

Stage B · stations and orbits

Blender work

New rock-and-ring station bases and debris built in Blender, status behaviour (thrusters, beacon, dim), orbit layout, hover labels.

Stage C · traffic and warp

The motion

Branching filaments, shuttles, the warp jump between projects, the drifting sky camera.

Notes

  • Sky photos would come from free sources, with credit in Settings › About. Candidates: NASA Goddard SVS "Deep Star Maps" and ESO's Milky Way panorama (CC BY 4.0). The licence is confirmed before anything is downloaded.
  • Frame rate is checked on the GTX 1650 after each stage. Lite quality keeps the sky photo (it is cheap) but drops flare, grain, debris and shuttles.
  • This page is a drawing to agree on the look. The real scene will look sharper (true 3D, real shadows) but follows the same layout and colours.
