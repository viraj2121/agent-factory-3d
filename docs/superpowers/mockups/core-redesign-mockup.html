The core: from loud to quiet and what to do with the rest of the scenery

The centre orb today is the loudest thing in the hall: a white-hot ball, three spinning rings, electric arcs, drifting sparks and a beam to the sky, all moving whatever the agents do. It says "something is happening" all the time, which means it says nothing. Below is today's core and five calmer redesigns, driven by the same real data (which agents work, which one waits for you). Switch the situation and compare.

Situation Six agents exist in this example. The core must show who works and who waits, and otherwise be quiet.

The rest of the scenery, in the same spirit

The rule: every moving thing must either mean something real, or be so quiet that you stop seeing it.

Element todayWhat it saysProposal
Four tool arms sweeping over the coreNothing real (decoration)Remove. The core stands alone.
Rocket build, welding robot, drone, countdown and lift-off loopPlan progress (when an agent wrote a plan), plus a lot of looping showKeep the rocket as a still monument whose sections show the plan's progress. No welding show, no drone, no loop.
Conveyor belt with two robots and moving cratesDevelopment is workingKeep, slower and smaller, and only moving while Development works. Remove the two belt robots.
Couriers and crawlers walking aboutNothing realRemove. Every walker looks like an agent and confuses the real ones.
Holograms, scanning screens, steam, sparks, floating dustNothing realKeep a few still, readable screens; remove steam, sparks and dust.
Ceiling light shaft, floor guide rings and emblemsNothing realThin, cool grey-blue lines; no shaft. Green and amber only for real status.
Space weather (meteors, ion storm)Project statusKeep, but only outside the dome and very faint. It is the project's mood, not its news.
Robots at desks, gems, tags, bubblesRealKeep and make the stars of the show.
